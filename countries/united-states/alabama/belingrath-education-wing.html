Bellingrath Education Wing

Established on June 9, 1946, the Bellingrath Education Building is a former Sunday school building located on the premises of the Memorial Presbyterian Church in Montgomery, Alabama. The first service was held on Mother’s day 1946. The land was donated by Mary Nesbitt Bellingrath, the widow of William Bellingrath, an elder in the Presbyterian and a philanthropist who frequently donated to institutions and charities. He frequently gave his time and means to the church and to the Presbyterian Home for Orphans at Talladega, before passing away on March 11, 1937.

The first building of the church to be constructed on the property was completed on December 22, 1947, costing approximately $15,000 (equivalent to approximately $225,000 in 2026). Originally small, the meeting house was expanded over the next several years to include a 600-seat sanctuary, meeting hall, Sunday school, and wedding chapel.

By 1952, the congregation had grown so much that it was time for a larger permanent sanctuary, designed by the same architects who had designed the original buildings, an architectural firm called Pearson and Tittle. Construction cost $300,000 (almost $3.8 million in 2026). The cornerstone was laid on February 27, 1955, containing the history of the church, roll of charter members, a bible, and other documents. The building was also equipped with a $30,000 organ donated by Mary Bellingrath, who passed away the following day.

The original building, located north of the sanctuary, was converted for educational purposes, containing nurseries and Sunday school classrooms. The two buildings were connected by a rear wing, which contained a fellowship hall and a kitchen. The wedding chapel was never built, although plans were drawn up.

The first wedding to take place in the sanctuary was that of Joseph and Junera Stone in 1955, and the first baptism was that of Stewart May. It was the first church in Montgomery to offer open seating to members of all races, in contrast to other similar churches in the South, which opposed desegregation and the Civil Rights Movement.

By the late 1990s, Memorial Presbyterian Church membership was declining, and they vacated the property, meeting at a local YMCA. The sanctuary is currently occupied by an active church (Saint James Baptist Church #2), but the Sunday School building is empty and heavily deteriorated.
